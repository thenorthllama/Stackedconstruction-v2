# Stacked Construction — Brand Voice & Messaging Guide

How we talk about what we do. Built on the StoryBrand (SB7) framework: *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*, Donald Miller.

**The one rule that governs everything below: the client is the hero. We are the guide.**
We are Yoda, not Luke. Every sentence on our site should sound like a trusted expert helping someone get somewhere — never like a company impressing you with itself.

## 2. Our BrandScript

### The Character (the hero — our client)

A Southwest Florida homeowner in Naples, Bonita Springs, Fort Myers, or Cape Coral. Often 35–65. Sometimes a primary residence, sometimes a seasonal home. They have taste, they have a budget, and they have a picture in their head of how they want to live.

**What they want:** a home that matches how they actually live — delivered without chaos.

### The Problem

| Level | What they're facing |
|---|---|
| **External** | "I know what I want my home to be, but I don't know how to get from an idea to a finished space — or who to trust with it." |
| **Internal** *(the one that actually drives the decision)* | "I'm afraid of being burned. Ghosted mid-project. Surprise change orders. Feeling stupid about decisions I don't understand. Ending up managing the project myself." |
| **Philosophical** | "A home this significant deserves a builder who respects it — and you. Nobody should have to chase a contractor to get what they paid for." |

**The villain we're fighting:** the black box. Contractors who go quiet, hide the numbers, and leave the homeowner guessing. We name that villain by contrast — we don't name competitors.

### The Guide (us) — empathy + competency, always in that order

**Empathy sounds like:**
- "You shouldn't have to chase anyone for an update."
- "We know what it takes to hand your home over to someone and hope for the best."
- "Most people we meet have a story about a contractor who let them down."

**Competency sounds like:**
- Licensed Florida Certified Building Contractor, **CBC1269972** (verifiable — we link to the state license lookup)
- 10+ years in the trades, 40+ completed projects across Lee and Collier counties
- Design and build under one roof — one team, one point of contact
- Named people on every project, not a call center
- Real photographs of finished work, never renderings or stock

Competency is stated plainly, once, and moved past. We never stack credentials for their own sake.

### The Plan (three steps — the same three everywhere)

1. **Consultation** — We walk your space, listen to how you want to live, and talk honestly about scope and budget.
2. **Design & Proposal** — We design it and hand you a line-item proposal. You see the whole picture before anyone picks up a tool.
3. **Build** — One team, one point of contact, a real schedule, and updates you don't have to ask for — through final walk-through.

**Our agreement plan (the promises behind the steps):**
- We show up when we say we will.
- You get a line-item proposal, not a lump sum.
- No surprise change orders — scope changes get priced and approved before work continues.
- We pull the permits and manage the inspections.
- We leave the site clean.
- Licensed and insured, and we'll hand you the documentation without being asked.

### Calls to Action

- **Direct CTA (one, everywhere, always identical):** **Schedule a Consultation**
- **Transitional CTA:** the planning guide / cost planning tool — for people not ready to talk yet.

Never use: "Learn more," "Get started," "Submit," "Click here." Those are suggestions, not invitations.

### The Stakes (what's at risk without a guide)

Months of delay. A budget that moves after work starts. A half-finished house you're living in. Unpermitted work you have to disclose when you sell. Stress that follows you home every evening. And in the worst case, paying twice to have it done right.

We state stakes plainly and briefly, near the top of a page — then swing immediately back to the positive. Never more than a few lines. We do not fearmonger.

### Success (where we take them)

A home that works the way they live — and that they're proud to host in. Permitted, warrantied, built for Florida's heat and humidity. A process they'd go through again, and a builder they'd hand a friend's phone number to.

### Identity Transformation

| From | To |
|---|---|
| A homeowner who's been let down before, or is afraid of it — hesitant to start something this big | A confident owner of a home they love, who knows they chose well |

## 3. Tone of voice

We sound like the most competent person in the room who has nothing to prove.

**Five attributes:**

1. **Calm.** No exclamation points, no urgency theater, no "act now." Confidence doesn't shout.
2. **Plain.** Short sentences. Real words. If a homeowner wouldn't say it out loud, we don't write it.
3. **Warm.** We're talking to a person about their home, which is personal. Direct, never cold.
4. **Specific.** "Handleless walnut cabinetry and a waterfall quartz island" beats "premium finishes." Specifics are how we prove competency without bragging.
5. **Honest.** We name real costs and real timelines. We'd rather lose a bid than win one on a number we can't hold.

**Luxury for us means restraint, not adjectives.** Our work looks expensive; the writing doesn't need to say so. Cut "stunning," "breathtaking," "dream," "world-class," "unparalleled," "state-of-the-art." The photography carries the glamour. The words carry the trust.

### Sentence-level rules

- **Second person.** "Your home," "you'll see," "we'll walk you through." Count the *you*s versus the *we*s — you should win.
- **Active voice.** "We pull the permits," not "permits are pulled."
- **Lead with the client's problem, then our solution.** Problem is the hook.
- **One idea per sentence.** Split anything with two commas and an "and."
- **Numbers over adjectives.** 40+ projects, 10+ years, CBC1269972, 6–10 weeks.
- **Em dashes sparingly** — one per paragraph at most.
- **No emoji. No all-caps for emphasis. No rhetorical questions as headlines** except where the client is genuinely asking it themselves.

### Words we use / words we don't

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Guide, walk you through, plan | Handle it for you, take it off your plate | They stay the hero; we stay the guide |
| Design-build, one team, one point of contact | Full service, turnkey solutions | Concrete beats jargon |
| Investment, scope, line-item proposal | Cheap, affordable, deal, discount | Price-shopper signals |
| Craftsmanship, built to last | Quality you can trust, second to none | Empty contractor clichés |
| Schedule a consultation | Get a free quote, learn more | Clear direct CTA |
| Licensed & insured · CBC1269972 | Fully licensed and bonded professionals | Specific and verifiable |
| Luxury (rarely, and only as a category) | Stunning, breathtaking, dream, elevate | Show, don't declare |

### Headline patterns that work for us

- **The client's outcome:** "A home that works the way you live."
- **The problem, then the turn:** "You shouldn't have to chase your contractor."
- **Plain statement of what we do:** "Design and build, under one roof."
- **Place + service, for local SEO pages:** "Kitchen Remodeling in Naples, FL"

Our display serif carries a two-tone headline: the main phrase in ink, one emphasized word in terracotta. Keep the emphasized word meaningful — a noun that carries the idea, never a filler word.

## 4. Page structure (the StoryBrand wireframe)

Every significant page follows this order. Miller's sequence, adapted to us.

1. **Header (above the fold)** — the offer in one line, a direct CTA, a transitional CTA. Very few words.
2. **The stakes** — briefly, what's at risk without a good builder.
3. **Value proposition** — three or four things they get, in plain bullets.
4. **The guide** — empathy line + competency proof (license, years, named team).
5. **The plan** — the same three steps: Consultation → Design & Proposal → Build.
6. **Explanatory paragraph** — for the people who need to do due diligence before calling.
7. **Success / vision** — what life looks like when it's finished, plus real work and real reviews.
8. **Direct CTA, repeated.**
9. **Footer ("junk drawer")** — everything else: license, service areas, links, hours.

**Above-the-fold rules:** an offer, not a mood. A button that leads to a transaction. Photographs of finished spaces, not of us or our trucks. If a stranger can't tell what we do, who it's for, and how to buy in five seconds, the header has failed the grunt test.

## 6. Quick self-check before publishing any page

- [ ] Would a stranger know what we do, who it's for, and how to buy — in five seconds?
- [ ] Is the client the hero, and are we clearly the guide?
- [ ] Does the page name a problem before it names a solution?
- [ ] Is there one empathy statement and one competency proof?
- [ ] Are the three plan steps present or linked?
- [ ] Is the direct CTA identical to every other page's, and repeated at least twice?
- [ ] Are the stakes stated once, briefly, without fearmongering?
- [ ] Is the success/vision specific rather than vague?
- [ ] More "you" than "we"?
- [ ] Can I cut half the words and lose nothing?
- [ ] Zero unearned adjectives?
- [ ] Every claim true and verifiable?
