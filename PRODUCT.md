# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Southwest Florida homeowners, 35-65, with $50K-$300K+ remodeling budgets. Making high-stakes, high-trust decisions about their primary asset. They've been burned before: a contractor who quoted and ghosted, a job left half-finished, a permit pulled wrong. They're not just buying a kitchen remodel. They're buying confidence that someone will actually show up, communicate, and finish what they started.

Context of use: researching on desktop or phone, often comparing 2-3 contractors, looking for signals of legitimacy and professionalism before making the call.

## Product Purpose

Convert homeowners into consultation bookings (phone call to (239) 785-0166 or contact form submission). Position Stacked Construction as the credible, premium alternative in a SWFL contractor market defined by flakiness, poor communication, and inconsistency.

Success: a homeowner finishes browsing and calls, or submits a form, without needing to look anywhere else.

## Brand Personality

Bold, confident, elevated. Not loud. The confidence of a firm that doesn't need to shout because the track record speaks. Warm but controlled. The luxury real estate developer energy, not the luxury interior design boutique energy.

## Anti-references

- Generic contractor site: stock photos of hard hats, blue or orange gradients, "Quality You Can Trust" banners, clip-art checkmarks.
- Houzz / home services aggregator: grid of project thumbnails, review stars everywhere, marketplace feel, comparison-shopping layout.
- Flashy SaaS startup: purple gradients, glowing CTAs, motion-heavy hero sections, animated counters.
- Interior design portfolio: precious, minimal, over-styled, prioritizes aesthetics over conversion. Beautiful but doesn't move people to act.

## Design Principles

1. **Reliability is the luxury.** In SWFL's contractor market, showing up and communicating is the differentiator. Every design choice should feel accountable and precise, not decorative. Trust signals (license numbers, named contractors, verifiable credentials) are design elements, not footnotes.
2. **Dark and cinematic, not dark and cold.** The warm neutral palette and terracotta accent carry heat through darkness. Luxury real estate editorial energy: think Related Companies or Toll Brothers prestige pages, not SaaS dark mode.
3. **Typography carries the voice.** Playfair Display italic delivers emotion; DM Sans delivers clarity. The hierarchy of the two fonts IS the brand personality in action. Never flatten it.
4. **CTAs invite, they don't beg.** The brand is confident enough not to be desperate. Calls to action are direct and certain, not anxious.
5. **Show the process, earn the trust.** Homeowners who've been burned want to see how the work gets done, not just the finished photos. Structure, timeline, accountability — making the invisible visible is a conversion tool.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: strong contrast ratios, keyboard navigation, screen reader support. No specific accommodation requirements stated. Note: junior bilingual (English/Portuguese) audience exists but no multilingual UI requirement at this time.
